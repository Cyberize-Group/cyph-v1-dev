2. WHAT WE BUILT AND LEARNED FROM THE FOUNDATION

We chose the cleaned Mission Control FFM as the starting shell because it already shared the product's appearance and Supabase authentication foundation. The generic starter would have required another cleanup; the Stripe demo remained a reference for later billing integration rather than the application's base.

Cleanup removed Mission Control-specific pages, mocks, services and types; introduced the portal shell and route group; moved reusable components and authentication helpers; and updated app identity, documentation and deployment scaffolding. The initial foundation commit reported in this lab was 1d15918.

A significant inherited defect survived the first cleanup: the cloned application still rejected admins because Mission Control was restricted to superadmins. The engineer corrected the login form, protected layout and login-page redirect logic to use one server-backed admin-or-superadmin access rule. A page-level guard also addressed protected dashboard content appearing in a redirect response. Tony then successfully tested a real admin login.

That was an interim access repair, not implementation of the final owner/store-manager model. Once admin means a store manager and superadmin means an account owner, the old admin-or-superadmin billing admission rule needs revision. A successful admin login must not later become evidence that every store manager is entitled to billing.

Lesson: when cloning an app, inspect its authorization assumptions, navigation, service names, deployment exclusions and recovery instructions as carefully as its visible pages.

4. THE MAJOR BUSINESS CHANGE: ONE OWNER, ONE SUBSCRIPTION, MULTIPLE STORES

Our working assumption had been one store per subscription, one admin per store, and superadmin reserved for our internal team. Coach's portal flow introduced an account subscription covering multiple pharmacies. That changes ownership, subscription attachment, invitations, access control and onboarding.

The new model separates account-wide authority from store-specific operations. One owner controls the account's subscription, billing and store additions. Each store has one designated manager/admin who manages that store and its members. The owner may also be a store's designated admin; this is the recommended answer to Coach's clarification and is already represented by the 'Me / Someone else' design. Record that answer explicitly rather than treating it as a separate approved stakeholder response.

Proposed role mapping for the new plan:

Super Admin / Account Owner — one customer account; manages billing, subscription, stores and store-admin assignments. Never implies platform-wide or cross-customer privilege.
Admin / Store Manager — assigned store; manages its operations and workers. Does not gain billing, subscription or account-wide store-creation authority.
Member / Store Worker — assigned store and approved worker capabilities. No billing or account ownership rights.
App Manager / Internal Operator — internal Mission Control operations across customer accounts, with explicitly bounded support capabilities and auditing. Subscription oversight does not automatically grant Stripe money access or PHI access.

These are four responsibility levels. They do not require four mutually exclusive global user labels. A person can own an account and also manage one of its stores. Account ownership and store membership need explicit scope. The exact tables, identifiers and policy functions must be designed after current-database recon.

Existing internal superadmin users must not silently become customer owners when the label is repurposed. Existing customer admins must not silently become owners either. Audit and map existing identities before migrating their permissions.

5. COACH'S REGISTRATION AND OWNERSHIP FEEDBACK

The original registration dropdown offered Owner, Pharmacist in charge, Manager and Other. These describe job titles, but the purchasing identity was going to receive account control. Tony highlighted the practical failure case: Frank owns seven stores; Heather registers with her email and pays using Frank's card; Heather's login could then control the whole subscription even though Frank supplies the money.

Coach approved owner-only registration, removal of the job-title dropdown, and the four-level access direction. He requested a required checkbox confirming that the registrant is the pharmacy owner, because software cannot infer ownership from a form submission.

Important correction for the next plan: email verification proves access to an inbox. Payment confirmation proves a payment outcome. Neither proves pharmacy ownership or Frank's authorization. Using Frank's card does not itself establish that he authorized Heather to own the account. The lean v1 approach is an explicit ownership declaration plus verified identity access, not a claim of independently verified legal ownership. Any stronger verification would be a separate product decision.

Coach also required a support-assisted ownership-transfer path for pharmacy sales and retirement; it need not be self-service in v1. The new plan should specify who authorizes transfer, which account/stores move, how old access is removed and how the change is audited. Do not implement it as an unrestricted edit to an email address.

Coach asked how many days the Phase 3 changes add. No defensible estimate was established in this lab. Targeted rework appears manageable, but current-schema and policy recon must precede a duration commitment. Retain applicable tenant-isolation and audit work rather than rebuilding Phase 3 indiscriminately.

6. STRIPE, ACTIVATION AND LOGIN

The public registration journey begins without signing in. After verified payment and account setup, the owner receives access. Returning authorized owners sign in to manage their subscription. Store-management invitations are distinct from the owner's account access and do not transfer billing authority.

Stripe Checkout is the hosted payment step for a new purchase. Stripe Customer Portal handles subsequent billing self-service such as payment methods and invoices, and configured subscription actions. They are different destinations. Our application creates the appropriate session; webhooks confirm and synchronize backend events. A webhook does not open the user's browser or launch a portal screen.

The FFM activation sequence demonstrated payment confirmation, pharmacy/account provisioning, then access-email delivery. The production plan must define separate states for payment pending/failed/confirmed, setup in progress/failed/completed, and email delivery failed/sent. Payment success with delayed provisioning must recover without charging again. Retried or out-of-order events must not create duplicate accounts, stores or invitations.

The existing plan specifies magic-link access after payment and rejects automatic login merely because the browser returned from Stripe. That is separate from an already authenticated owner moving between OwedBook and the billing app. Shared sessions remain an intended experience, not something established solely by using one Supabase database or deploying under sibling subdomains. Test the actual cross-app auth flow later; Mission Control retains a separate internal access boundary.

NEW REQUIREMENT REPORTED BY TONY: Frank wants BOTH passwordless magic-link login AND Google OAuth 2.0 login. Record this now for the authentication workstream; implementation is a later concern, not a reason to reopen the deployed FFM.

The later auth scope must define first access, returning-user sign-in, invitation acceptance, provider identity linking, expired/used links, approved redirect destinations, and whether email/password remains available. Google sign-in must resolve an authorized account and its entitlements; it must not create a paid subscription or bypass owner registration. Keep this separate from deciding which roles can access which resources.

7. COACH AND FRANK: PHARMACY SOFTWARE AND V1 LIMITS

Coach explicitly set two v1 data paths: a Liberty connection and manual file upload. Pioneer, DRX and Rx30 automated connections are outside this v1 scope. Customers using those systems or 'Other' use manual upload. Billing starts at purchase, and pharmacy-software choice does not block checkout or trigger a prepayment compatibility review.

This reverses our earlier proposed compatibility gate. The new plan should follow Coach's ruling, while clearly explaining the supported import method before payment. A software-selection dropdown must not imply that every named vendor has a working automatic connection.

Manual upload still needs a defined supported format. 'Available to everyone' cannot mean 'every arbitrary CSV/XLSX file works.' Decide which source exports are supported or provide a canonical template with mapping guidance. Define validation, rejected-row feedback, duplicate prevention and a sample-file acceptance suite.

Frank suggested screenshots/videos for API-key setup for Liberty and DRX. Guidance for the in-scope Liberty connection can be considered within its onboarding design; DRX integration remains outside the agreed v1 connector scope.

Frank also suggested letting unpaid prospects connect/import data to demonstrate potential recovery. That is a separate acquisition model, requiring prepayment identities, data permissions, retention/deletion rules, abuse controls, usage limits and conversion behavior. It is outside current v1 scope. Record it as a future proposal rather than letting it enter through a small onboarding change. The existing plan already mentions trial mechanics, so reconcile or defer those explicitly instead of silently assuming a free data-analysis trial is authorized.

Coach left setup responsibility, required customer inputs, pre-import experience and mixed import methods across stores for us to propose. Recommended direction: configure data connection per store, provide a clear empty state before first import, and allow stores to use supported methods independently. These remain proposals pending acceptance.

8. CLAIM DATA, REFERENCE DATA AND GOLDEN DATA ARE DIFFERENT

Pharmacy-system exports or APIs provide the claim transactions: drug identifiers, quantities, fill dates, payer information and amounts paid. Pricing/reference feeds provide the benchmark inputs and classifications used by the calculation engine. OwedBook combines the two according to the approved math specification. A prescription document uploaded by a patient is not established as a required input in this workflow.

The golden dataset is controlled validation evidence with expected results or reviewed outcomes. It is neither a live customer feed nor the production drug-price source.

The math knowledge base records public AAC/FUL sources for historical validation and a commercial reference-vendor decision involving Medi-Span/Elsevier for production. Do not assume that choosing or paying a vendor makes every field authoritative for every calculation. Confirm the licensed datasets, units, historical coverage, update process and precedence. Preserve append-only versions and the reference provenance of each calculated claim.

Two document conflicts need explicit reconciliation. First, the math knowledge base describes Liberty API, DRX API, Pioneer SFTP and Rx30 upload; Coach's later v1 ruling narrows automated integration to Liberty. Preserve the other information as future integration knowledge, not current delivery scope. Second, the eight-phase plan still lists five blocking math rulings as outstanding; the math knowledge base records them as resolved, with additional questions such as the U&C fee treatment remaining open. Carry forward the actual current ruling register rather than asking Frank the same settled questions again.

10. LESSONS FROM REVIEW, QA AND DEPLOYMENT

The FFM proved valuable because it exposed ownership, billing and onboarding ambiguities before live money and provisioning were connected. Tony's manual exploration was a requirements-discovery activity as well as product acceptance.

Demo controls must be understandable. 'Next activation check' appeared ineffective to the reviewer and reset to ready; controls that silently affect a later step need clear labeling or a simpler scenario mechanism. Do not expose internal testing vocabulary as customer-facing product behavior.

Copy must match the actual state. 'Payment wasn't completed' contradicts 'we are checking whether payment went through.' Pending confirmation needs neutral language and a blocked duplicate-payment attempt. Quote expiry and saved-signup retention are separate concepts; an 'advance 31 minutes' test control does not settle the business retention policy.

Keep provisional pricing, cancellation policy, grace periods, branding and support/legal links visibly provisional until approved. A mock rule such as pausing store additions during scheduled cancellation must not become an unreviewed production policy.

QA closeout preserves the decision and accepted observations. Documentation-only closeout followed by matching build-input bytes does not require another routine product campaign. Actual product changes require proportionate affected verification. Do not remove harmless unused exports after certification merely to tidy the code.

The deployment workflow initially behaved like a long manual walkthrough. Tony redirected it toward the intended automation: inspect what exists, prepare one discoverable configuration file, explain only missing values, guide user-only authentication, then execute approved work and record evidence. The reusable procedure must carry this behavior so a second architect is not needed to interpret every deployment.

Operational lessons included preserving useful Docker exclusions, excluding documentation/test/auth artifacts from uploads, verifying upload contents despite broken local symlinks, checking active cloud identity/project, using a distinct service per independent app, and distinguishing DNS setup from Cloud Run domain mapping and HTTPS readiness. Domain login redirects must be configured when magic-link/OAuth flows are introduced; successful email/password login alone does not prove those flows work.
